Coverage and missing conflicts

The initial source is Tony Jaques’s Dictionary of Battles and Sieges (2007). Its preface generally excludes events preserved only in oral history and acknowledges omissions in pre-European sub-Saharan Africa. Counts reflect the source’s coverage as well as history. Regional histories and archaeological evidence will be needed to broaden the dataset.

Dates, locations, and borders

Entries are shown whenever their recorded year interval overlaps the selected years. A year range may describe a siege, a combined entry, or an uncertain date; it does not establish continuous fighting. Coordinates are approximate reference points, sometimes town or lake centers. Modern country outlines provide orientation and do not represent medieval borders. Unresolved entries stay in the list without a map point.

Deaths and dot size

Dots currently have equal size. This does not imply equal severity. Death estimates are unvalidated; missing values are unknown, not zero. Future estimates should distinguish military and civilian deaths from wounded, captured, or missing people, preserve alternative sources and ranges, and scale dot area to the chosen measure.

Sources and reuse

Every entry retains its source and page references. Coordinates are provisionally sourced from Wikipedia GeoData or Wikidata; the map uses @d3-maps/atlas 1.0.0 country geometry. These sources have their own terms. The original book and its full entry text are not included. Public availability does not make this a validated or uniformly licensed dataset.
